## Formats are the first filter

A cookie plant can bake a biscuit; it cannot freeze-dry raw chicken. Before you contact anyone, name your format honestly: baked biscuit, soft-baked or soft chew, dehydrated jerky, freeze-dried, or extruded. Most searches for a "dog treat co-packer" turn up plants that do one or two of these. Baked treats tend to have the most options, because the equipment overlaps with human bakery lines.

## The regulatory questions

Pet food, treats included, is regulated as animal food. Ask:

- **Is the plant registered with FDA as a food facility,** and does it follow FDA's animal food rules ([21 CFR part 507](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-507): current good manufacturing practice and preventive controls)?
- **Does its labeling team know pet-food labels:** the guaranteed analysis, the ingredient statement, and the naming rules in AAFCO's model regulations, which state label rules largely follow?
- **Who handles state registrations?** States regulate pet food sales, treats included, through product registration, company licensing, or both ([AAFCO](https://www.aafco.org/resources/startups/registration-licensing/)). A co-packer that has done it before saves you time.
- **If it also runs human food, how does it separate pet and human production?**

## Human grade is a specific claim

"Human grade" is not marketing language you can add to a bag. Under the [AAFCO standard](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/AAFCO_Human_Grade_Standards_AMS.pdf), the product and every ingredient must be stored, handled, processed, and transported in line with human-food manufacturing rules, and the product must be made in a facility licensed to make human food. A plant licensed only for animal food cannot support the claim, so if it matters to your brand, raise it on the first call.

## The questions for the first call

1. Which of my formats can you run today, and what would need new equipment?
2. What is the minimum per SKU, and can the first run be smaller?
3. How long from approved sample to first run, and from purchase order to ship on repeats?
4. Which certifications do you hold, and will you send the certificates?
5. How do you handle protein sourcing, allergens, and cross-contact between SKUs?
6. Can you support a human-grade claim, if I need one?
7. Who owns a recipe you develop for me?
8. Who do I call when a run goes wrong?

## Listings raise the stakes

A first listing with an online pet retailer or a regional pet chain is where home-kitchen brands break. The reorder arrives faster than the first order did, and the plant has to be ready. Line up a co-packer before the listing goes live, not after the first stockout. For the paperwork buyers ask for, see [what big retailers require from a new supplier](/guides/what-retailers-require-from-a-new-supplier/).

## Sample before you sign

Send a case of your treats. A good plant benchmarks them, returns a sample with a spec sheet, and quotes from that. Test the sample with your own dog and a few others, check how it holds up after a few weeks in the bag, and compare the guaranteed analysis with your current label. If a plant will not sample before quoting, keep looking.
