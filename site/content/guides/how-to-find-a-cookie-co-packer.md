## Where cookie co-packers are listed

There is no single directory, but four places cover most of the US market.

- **Co-manufacturer marketplaces.** [Keychain](https://www.keychain.com/) and [PartnerSlate](https://www.partnerslate.com/) let you describe your product and get matched with manufacturers that say they can make it. They are the fastest way to build a long list.
- **Trade associations and directories.** The [Specialty Food Association](https://www.specialtyfood.com/) and the supplier directories that trade publications publish list plants by category.
- **The people who sell to bakeries.** Packaging reps, ingredient distributors, and food brokers visit plants every week and know which ones have room this quarter. Ask them first; it is faster than any list.
- **Other founders.** Brands one step ahead of you know which plants answered the phone and which ones quoted and disappeared.

## Qualify before you call

Most wasted calls come from a mismatch you could have caught on the website. Check three things first.

1. **Format.** Drop cookies, wire-cut cookies, bars, sandwich cookies, and soft-baked cookies run on different equipment. A plant that runs your format today is a much shorter path than one that would need new equipment.
2. **Shelf life.** Is your cookie shelf-stable, refrigerated, or frozen? A plant built for one rarely does the others as well.
3. **Scale.** If a site advertises "small batch" and your retailer needs pallets every week, move on. If it lists only national brands, ask whether it takes new ones.

## The eight questions for the first call

1. **What formats and pack sizes do you run today?** The answer tells you whether your cookie needs new equipment.
2. **What is the minimum run per SKU, and can the first run be different?** Minimums come from line economics, and a plant with open capacity has more reason to make a first run work. See [co-packer minimums, lead times, and costs](/guides/co-packer-minimums-lead-times-and-costs/).
3. **What is the lead time from purchase order to ship, on the first run and on repeats?**
4. **Which certifications do you hold, and will you send the certificates?** Ask for documents, not descriptions. Retail buyers will; see [what big retailers require from a new supplier](/guides/what-retailers-require-from-a-new-supplier/).
5. **How do you handle allergens?** Shared lines with a cleaning program, or dedicated lines; the answer changes what your label can say.
6. **How does sampling work, and when would I have a sample?** A date is a good answer. "After we clear the backlog" is not.
7. **Who owns the recipe if you develop it?** Get this in writing before formulation starts.
8. **Who do I call when a run goes wrong?** A named person, not a portal.

## What to send after the call

Your product (a case, not a bag), the spec if you have one, your target retail price, and a realistic monthly volume for the next twelve months. A good co-packer benchmarks the product, sends a sample back with a spec sheet, and quotes from that. If a plant quotes before it has tasted anything, treat the number as a guess.

## How to compare quotes

Put every quote on the same basis before you compare them.

- **What the unit price includes:** ingredients, packaging, labor, and case packing, or only some of them.
- **One-time costs:** formulation, sampling, setup, and printing plates for custom film or cartons.
- **Minimums and price breaks:** the price at your first-run volume and at the volume you expect in a year.
- **Freight:** where the product ships from and who books it.

## The most common mistake

Choosing on price per unit alone. The cost that hurts is the reorder you cannot fill because the plant is full. A slightly higher unit price from a plant with capacity beats a lower one from a plant that puts you at the back of the line.
