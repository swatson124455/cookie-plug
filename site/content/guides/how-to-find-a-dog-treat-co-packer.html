title: How to Find a Dog Treat Co-Packer: Formats, Certifications, and the Questions That Matter description: A guide for pet-treat brands moving from a home kitchen or a first manufacturer to a co-packer: baked versus dehydrated versus soft chews, AAFCO and state registration, and how to qualify a plant. date: 2026-10-01

Short answer: match the plant to your format first (baked biscuits, soft-baked, dehydrated jerky, extruded kibble, and freeze-dried are different equipment), then confirm it understands pet-food regulation (AAFCO labeling, state feed registrations, FDA facility registration), then ask about minimums, lead time, and sampling. A baked-treat plant with open capacity can sample in weeks.

Formats are the first filter

A cookie plant can bake a biscuit; it cannot freeze-dry raw chicken. Before contacting anyone, name your format honestly: baked biscuit, soft chew, dehydrated meat, extruded, or freeze-dried. Most "dog treat co-packer" searches lead to plants that do one or two of these. Baked treats have the most options and the lowest minimums because the equipment overlaps with human bakery lines.

The regulatory questions

Pet food is regulated as animal feed. Ask whether the plant is FDA-registered as a food facility, whether its labeling team knows AAFCO nutrient profiles and guaranteed-analysis panels, and who handles state feed registrations (each state requires its own, and a co-packer that has done it before saves you months). If the plant also runs human food, ask how it separates pet and human production on shared lines.

The questions that matter on the call

  1. Which of my format can you run today, and what would need new equipment?
  2. What is the minimum per SKU, and can the first run be smaller?
  3. How long from approved sample to first run, and from PO to ship on repeats?
  4. Which certifications do you hold, with documents?
  5. How do you handle protein sourcing and allergens across SKUs?
  6. Can you produce a human-grade claim, and what does that require?
  7. Who owns a recipe you develop for me?

Chewy, Petco, and the reorder

A first listing on Chewy or a regional Petco rollout is where home-kitchen brands break. The reorder arrives faster than the first order did, and the plant has to be ready. Line up a co-packer before the listing goes live, not after the first stockout.

Sample before you sign

Send a case of your treats. A good plant benchmarks them, returns the sample with a spec sheet, and quotes from that. Judge the sample with your own dog and three others. If the plant will not sample before quoting, keep looking.
