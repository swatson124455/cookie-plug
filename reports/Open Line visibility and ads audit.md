# Borrow the Directories' Authority, Then Buy Search

Open Line should not try to outrank the directories for "cookie co-packer" in its first year. It should get listed inside them, win the informational long tail they ignore, and build the off-site mentions that AI answer engines actually reward. Page one for bakery and pet head terms belongs to aged lists and marketplaces: pickyourown.org, specialtyfoodcopackers.com, PartnerSlate, Keychain, and state and extension pages. But the questions founders ask next ("what is a normal minimum", "what does a co-packer charge per unit", "commissary or co-packer first", "small-batch co-packer") are answered today by beverage blogs, repack pricing pages and sauce forums. A bakery- and pet-specific site with sourced numbers can win those. On AI visibility, the site's on-page work (answer-first guides, citations, JSON-LD) is already ahead of most competitors. The things that now hold it back are off the site. The site must be indexed in Google, Bing and OpenAI's own crawler. It also needs brand mentions: across 75,000 brands, mentions correlated 0.664 with AI Overview visibility, against 0.218 for backlinks. For paid traffic, Google and then Microsoft search are the only channels worth the first dollar. Expect roughly $3 to $9 a click. New-advertiser credits of $500 to $1,000 from Google and $500 from Microsoft can double a $300 to $600 test. The 90-day plan at the end spends nothing until a defined proof gate. It keeps the owner's visibility work to about 8 to 10 of their 20 weekly hours and moves every buildable task into the site. It also treats the tracking tag as a deferred decision, because at the expected lead volume the tag's main benefit, automated bidding, would not switch on inside 90 days anyway.

One caveat applies to everything below. The competitive picture comes from a US web-search tool run on September 30, 2026, not from live Google results. No search volumes, keyword difficulty or backlink data were available, and no ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot or Google AI Mode sessions could be run. Treat the rankings as the pool of pages a search engine retrieves, not as exact positions.

## Directories own the head terms; nobody owns the follow-up questions

Page one splits three ways. Directories and lists hold the cookie, bakery and "near me" queries. Manufacturers' own service pages hold the dog-treat and pet-treat queries. Blogs from co-packers, SaaS tools and consultancies hold the informational queries. For "cookie co-packers near me", **pickyourown.org alone took five of ten results** with its US bakery, master, California, Montana and Pennsylvania lists ([pickyourown](https://pickyourown.org/list_of_copackers.htm)). These lists are simple directories of names, locations and phone numbers ([pickyourown](https://www.pickyourown.org/copackers-US-Bakeries.php)). PartnerSlate appears for both the bakery and pet-food head terms and advertises "553 bakery manufacturers" actively sourcing projects ([PartnerSlate](https://partnerslate.com/bakery/)). Keychain runs programmatic "N Best … Manufacturers" pages by cookie subtype and by city or state, for example Texas, Chicago and Florida ([Keychain](https://www.keychain.com/manufacturer/private-label-cookies)). For "private label dog treats", year-stamped aggregators lead: "Top 100 … in 2026" and "15 Best … (2026)" ([ensun](https://ensun.io/search/private-label-dog-treat), [usetorg](https://usetorg.com/category/private-label-dog-treats)). Behind them sits a crowd of individual manufacturers such as PetDine, Natural Pet Innovations, KCC and Packing Paws ([Packing Paws](https://packingpaws.com/)).

The incumbents are less formidable than their positions suggest. The same domains take several slots on one query, so a site with one page per intent has fewer real rivals than ten results imply. Several ranking pages are visibly neglected. PetDine ranks a URL literally named "services-old" ([PetDine](https://www.petdinellc.com/services-old/all-natural-dog-treat-manufacturing/)), and Not Just Cookies ranks a page slugged "copy-of-corporate-ordering" ([Not Just Cookies](https://notjustcookies.com/pages/copy-of-corporate-ordering)). The pet-food results lean on a Packaging World trend piece and a 2021 B&W Packaging post ([Packworld](https://www.packworld.com/trends/contract-packaging/article/21927766/pet-food-copackers-are-indemand), [B&W](https://www.bwpackaging.com/blog/post/blog/2021/09/10/planning-capital-costs-in-pet-food-packaging)). Almost no dedicated pet-food manufacturer page ranks. That is the thinnest manufacturer presence of any head term tested.

The follow-up queries show the real gap. The results for "co-packer minimum order quantity" are mostly beverage pages, and the generic figure that ranks is "5,000 to 50,000 units depending on complexity" ([DocShipper](https://docshipper.com/glossary/co-packer-definition-logistics/), [RapidCPG](https://rapidcpg.com/blog/beverage-minimum-order-quantities)). For "co-packer cost per unit", the ranking pages quote contract-packaging rates such as "$0.25–$0.60 per unit" for repack and kitting ([MSL Indy](https://msl-indy.com/co-packing-pricing/), [Industrial Packaging](https://www.industrialpackaging.com/blog/contract-packaging-cost)). Those are not bake or extrusion costs. For "small batch co-packer", sauce forums and beverage co-packers rank, and no bakery or pet page appears ([TheHotPepper](https://thehotpepper.com/threads/im-a-small-batch-low-minimum-order-copacker-ask-me-anything.77523/), [Vital Eats](https://www.vitaleats.com/co-packing-faq)). The "commissary vs co-packer" pages come from kitchen-rental operators who have a stake in the answer. One advises "try the kitchen first" ([Ardent Seller](https://www.ardentseller.app/blog/co-packer-vs-shared-commercial-kitchen), [Prep Kitchens](https://prepkitchens.com/commercial-kitchens-vs-co-packer-which-on-should-you-choose-first/)). Gold Medal Bakery shows that a bakery-specific blog can compete on generic queries: its "How to Find a Co-Packer" post ranks next to Food Business News and university extension pages ([Gold Medal](https://www.goldmedalbakery.com/blog/how-to-find-a-co-packer/)).

| Query cluster | Who holds it today | Winnable for a new domain? |
|---|---|---|
| cookie / bakery / baked goods co-packer, "near me" | pickyourown, specialtyfoodcopackers, mass.gov, Cornell, PartnerSlate, YouBar, Gold Medal | Hard in year one; get listed inside these instead |
| dog treat co-packer, private label dog treats, pet treat co-manufacturer | ensun, usetorg, many thin manufacturer pages | Moderate; manufacturers have a trust edge (address, certifications) |
| pet food co-packer | Packaging World, Medium, The Underbite, PartnerSlate, old blogs | Winnable; the site has a line page but no guide yet |
| co-packer MOQ; cost per unit | Beverage and repack blogs | Most winnable with bakery- and pet-specific ranges |
| small batch / low minimums | Sauce forums, beverage co-packers | Most winnable, but only with sourced industry ranges, never facility claims |
| commissary kitchen to co-packer; shelf-stable cookies | Kitchen-rental operators; directories plus one manufacturer | Most winnable; existing guides already target them |

That table sets the content agenda. The eight existing guides already cover minimums and costs, the commissary exit and shelf-stable conversion. Five gaps remain, and nothing strong ranks for any of them. The first is a "questions to ask a bakery or pet-treat co-packer before signing" guide covering formula ownership, NDAs and agreements. Today only UK sites, a Canadian government PDF and generic US pages rank for it ([SimplePack](https://simplepack.co.uk/25-essential-questions-to-ask-a-co-packer-before-signing-a-contract/), [Manitoba](https://www.gov.mb.ca/agriculture/food-and-ag-processing/starting-a-food-business/pubs/af-resource-guide-for-co-packing-legal-aspects.pdf)). The second is a pet-food co-packer guide. The third explains what a cookie or dog-treat quote actually includes. The fourth is private label versus co-packing for dog treats. The fifth covers small-batch and pilot runs. Two line-page additions copy a tactic that works for competitors: manufacturers hold two slots with separate co-packing and private-label pages ([Gold Medal](https://www.goldmedalbakery.com/services/private-label/)), and individually wrapped, shelf-stable cookies are a niche with its own ranking page ([WOW Baking](https://wowbaking.com/pages/shelf-stable-individually-wrapped)). The line-page additions are a private-label section on the dog-treat page and a shelf-stable, individually wrapped section on the cookie page.

The facility-fact rule shapes all of this. The small-batch guide and the MOQ tables must present labeled, sourced industry ranges, not what the partner plant can do. Minimums, lead times and certifications stay out until `config/facility.yaml` confirms them. This limits the site less than it seems. The directory snippets that search summaries quote are concrete numbers, such as "pilot run MOQs starting at 500 LBs" ([specialtyfoodcopackers](https://specialtyfoodcopackers.com/Cookies.html)). A sourced industry range does the same job without making a claim about the facility. Local "near me" is not worth fighting. A referral site has no plant address to show, so the local results will go to plants regardless.

## AI engines reward mentions and freshness, not more markup

No published study covers AI citations for co-packing questions, so the best evidence is what a live search retrieves. For "how to find a cookie co-packer", the synthesized answer recommended directories and process steps rather than naming a manufacturer. It drew on pickyourown, UC Davis, PartnerSlate, Gold Medal and Specialty Food Resource ([UC Davis](https://ucfoodsafety.ucdavis.edu/processing-distribution/food-industry-contacts/co-packers), [Specialty Food Resource](https://www.specialtyfoodresource.com/find-a-business-resource/copackers/)). For "dog treat co-packer low minimums", it pulled small co-packers' own pages. Neoteric claims orders "as small as 150 units", and PacknFresh and The Underbite supplied the MOQ tiers ([Neoteric](https://www.neotericbrands.com/blogs/general/private-label-dog-treats-your-guide-to-pet-product-manufacturing), [PacknFresh](https://packnfresh.com/pet-food-co-packing/)). For pricing, it quoted GhostLabel's setup fees of $250 to $1,500 per run ([GhostLabel](https://www.ghostlabel.io/blog/how-much-do-food-co-packers-charge)). The pattern favors a new site: the winners are small vendors that state specific numbers, not big brands. The same pattern shows where AI answers find named co-packers, which is directory pages. SpecialtyFoodCoPackers even runs a page titled for co-packers with additional capacity ([SpecialtyFoodCoPackers](https://www.specialtyfoodcopackers.com/CoPackers-Additional-Capacity.html)). That page is an exact fit for a plant with open lines. Getting listed there is likely a faster route into AI answers than the site ranking on its own. That is a judgment, not a measurement.

Broader studies point in the same direction. In Ahrefs' 75,000-brand analysis, **branded web mentions correlated 0.664 with AI Overview visibility versus 0.218 for backlinks** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). A follow-up extended to ChatGPT and AI Mode found **YouTube mentions were the strongest signal at about 0.737** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260526119691/en/Across-75000-Brands-YouTube-Mentions-Are-the-Strongest-Signal-of-AI-Visibility-New-Ahrefs-Report-Reveals)). Both are correlations, not proof of cause. Citation shares skew by engine. Wikipedia leads ChatGPT at 7.8% of citations, and Reddit leads Perplexity at 6.6% ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)). AI Overviews favor YouTube and Reddit, and ChatGPT's top sources include Medium ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-search-engines-cite-reddit-youtube-and-linkedin-most-study-473138)). For professional and B2B queries, a PR-agency synthesis ranks LinkedIn first across six AI platforms ([5W via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/5w-releases-ai-platform-citation-source-index-2026-the-50-websites-that-now-decide-what-brands-are-visible-inside-chatgpt-claude-perplexity-gemini-and-google-ai-overviews-302759804.html)). Its methodology is unverified. Freshness counts too. AI-cited pages average about 25.7% fresher than organic results, and ChatGPT cites pages 393 to 458 days newer than organic ones ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-seo-statistics/)). The one controlled test found that adding statistics, quotations and source citations lifted visibility in generative answers by roughly 30 to 40% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735)), though it ran on 2023–24 systems. The guides already carry cited numbers. The gains still available are keeping them dated and refreshed, and getting the brand named elsewhere.

Indexing is the prerequisite most likely to be missed. ChatGPT is building its own index. A reverse-engineering study found that only **1.5% of URLs from OpenAI's index appeared in Bing's top 20** for the same searches, so Bing visibility is no longer a safe proxy for ChatGPT ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/chatgpt-built-its-own-search-index)). OpenAI says sites that block OAI-SearchBot won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers, and that setting is independent of GPTBot, its training crawler ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). Anthropic runs separate tokens for ClaudeBot (training), Claude-User and Claude-SearchBot ([Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler)). PerplexityBot surfaces sites in Perplexity results and is not used for training ([Perplexity](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)). The site's `User-agent: * / Allow: /` robots file already admits all of them. The open risks are two. The draft build's sitemap line still points at example.com. And Netlify or any CDN bot protection could block these agents without anyone noticing. Copilot grounds on Bing. Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report, released in February 2026, shows Copilot citations, cited pages and grounding queries, and Citation Share was added in June ([Bing](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c)). Microsoft's Fabrice Canel said generative AI values fresh content and recommended IndexNow to push updates ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-copilot-use-schema-for-its-llms-453455)). IndexNow is cheap to build into the static deploy. It also pays off every month when the dated capacity line changes.

Two items deserve less effort than they get. Google's June 2026 documentation update says llms.txt files "won't harm (nor help)" and that Google Search does not use them ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/ai-search/google-llms-txt-no-ranking-benefit-june-2026)). In a 12-week log study of 83 sites, OpenAI fetched llms.txt 7 times and Perplexity never did ([ezy.ai](https://www.ezy.ai/research/do-ai-bots-read-llms-txt)). Keep the file, since it costs nothing, but stop working on it. Schema should stay accurate, but it will not decide the outcome. Google says AI features need no special markup ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), while Microsoft says schema helps Copilot understand pages ([SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/schema-llms-copilot-bing-microsoft-39093.html)). One unknown remains unresolved: no evidence was found on whether answer engines discount a referral site relative to a manufacturer. The owner should measure rather than assume. That means running five fixed buyer prompts once a month in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Google AI Mode and Claude, logging which URLs each engine cites, and reading the Bing AI Performance data alongside.

## Search ads at $3 to $9 a click are the only paid test worth running first

No published CPC exists for "co-packer", "bakery co-packer" or "private label dog treats". The owner must pull those numbers from Google Keyword Planner, which is free with an account ([Google](https://business.google.com/us/ad-tools/keyword-planner/)), and from Microsoft's planner. The closest published keyword figure is **"food product manufacturers" at $8.63** ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/industries/food-beverage/food-beverage-manufacturing/ppc/)). The WordStream/LocaliQ 2026 benchmarks cover 13,474 US search campaigns. They put Industrial & Commercial at a $5.87 CPC, an 8.20% conversion rate and a $75.19 cost per lead. Business Services converts at 4.85% with a $93.69 cost per lead ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks)). Animals & Pets runs $4.06 a click and Restaurants & Food $2.05, but both are mostly consumer advertisers and understate B2B costs ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/)). A working range of $3 to $9 a click is therefore reasonable. The binding limit is more likely search volume than price. At $10 a day on exact and phrase match, the budget may not fully spend, and loosening to broad match to spend it would waste the money. Cost trends favor Google right now. Google Search CPCs rose about 1% year over year in Q2 2026, while Microsoft's rose 19% ([Tinuiti via Karooya](https://www.karooya.com/blog/digital-ads-benchmark-report-by-tinuiti-q2-2026-key-highlights/)). Microsoft still runs about a third cheaper per click ([Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/bing-ads-vs-google-ads)). It is also the only platform besides LinkedIn that can layer LinkedIn company, industry and job-function data onto search, as a bid-only adjustment ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/msa-help/hlp_ba_conc_linkedintargeting)).

Every other channel reaches people who are not looking for a co-packer, and most cost more. LinkedIn has a $10-a-day minimum and a typical CPC of $4.50 to $15 ([Flying V Group](https://www.flyingvgroup.com/linkedin-ads-minimum-budget/), [ClickMinded](https://www.clickminded.com/linkedin-ads-benchmarks/)). That would spend a whole $300 month on roughly 20 to 65 clicks. Reddit is cheap at a $1.25 to $1.85 median CPC, but campaigns under about $20 a day struggle to deliver ([Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/reddit-ads/reddit-ads-cost-benchmarks), [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/reddit-ads-minimum-budget-requirements-2026)). Meta's $27.39 average cost per lead looks attractive ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/)). But Meta removed detailed-targeting exclusions and now treats interests as suggestions under Advantage+ ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-removes-more-detailed-ad-targeting-options-facebook-instagram/757856/)), so a co-packing ad will reach hobby bakers and pet owners. The cheapest-first order is therefore: Google Search, then Microsoft Search, then retargeting once traffic exists, then Reddit community and keyword targeting, then Meta lead ads, with LinkedIn last.

New-advertiser credits change the arithmetic, but sources disagree on the terms. A limited-time Google offer reported in May 2026 pays **$2 of credit per $1 spent in the first 60 days, so $500 of spend unlocks $1,000** ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/google-ads-2x-credit-new-accounts/)). Google's standing Partner tiers start at 1:1, so $500 of spend earns $500 ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15329625?hl=en-AU)). Microsoft reports spend-$250-get-$500 for new US accounts, and the credit expires 90 days after it is awarded ([Adcore](https://www.adcore.com/blog/microsoft-ads-credits-how-new-users-claim-free/)). Credit posts about 30 to 35 days after the spend threshold is met, and sources conflict on whether eligibility runs 20 days from account creation or 14 days from first impression ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6388096?hl=en)). That conflict leads to a practical rule that follows from the documented terms: **do not create the Google Ads account just to browse Keyword Planner weeks before spending.** Open it in the week paid search launches, pull planner data first, and read the offer shown in the account. Until then, Search Console impressions give free, real query data. Google Ad Grants is for nonprofits and does not apply.

The cost controls are standard. The notes' guidance on this is clear. Use exact and phrase match only, with Manual CPC or Maximize Clicks capped around $8. Set up separate bakery/cookie and pet treat/pet food ad groups, each pointed at its line page. Target people located in the US, not people merely interested in it, and schedule weekday business hours. Load a negative list from day one covering jobs and careers, recipes, homemade and DIY, consumer shopping terms (amazon, chewy, buy, coupon), equipment and packaging supplies, and off-category terms such as cannabis, beverage, supplements and cosmetics. Negatives do not match close variants, so add both singular and plural forms ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en)). Ad copy can say the lines are open, since that fact is confirmed monthly in `site/config.yaml`. It cannot state a minimum or lead time until `config/facility.yaml` confirms one.

| Plan (estimates) | Spend | Clicks/month | Form leads/month | Qualified leads/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Google Search only, two ad groups | $300 ($10/day) | 33–100 | ~1–6 | ~0–4 |
| B: Google $300 + Microsoft $150 (LinkedIn industry bid-only) + Reddit $150 probe | $600 | ~95–475 | ~1.5–16 | ~1–7 |

These figures rest on the benchmark CPCs above and an assumed 2 to 6% conversion rate. That rate is set below the 4.85 to 8.2% benchmarks because the site is an unknown brand selling a high-consideration B2B service, so the first 60 days of real data should replace it. The better variant of Plan B drops Reddit, whose $5 a day sits below stable delivery, and runs Google $450 plus Microsoft $150. Two decision rules keep the test honest. Keep a channel if cost per qualified lead stays under about $300, given that one closed account is worth thousands to tens of thousands a year. And if 100 or more targeted clicks produce zero leads, fix the page or the offer before adding spend.

### The tracking tag is a real decision, and deferring it costs little

The Netlify form already lands on /thanks/, which is the most reliable conversion trigger. A page view of a thank-you URL only happens after a real submission ([Analytics Mania](https://www.analyticsmania.com/thank-you-page-tracking-google-tag-manager/)). Counting it inside Google or Microsoft, though, means shipping their JavaScript. The plumbing exists: `analytics_snippet` and `analytics_hosts` in `site/config.yaml`, with the CSP builder in `src/leadgen/website/seo.py` adding the tag's origins and inline-script hashes automatically. Using it ends the no-JavaScript rule, and that call belongs to the owner.

| Option | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Stay tag-free: noindex landing pages per channel (for example /lp/google-bakery) with a hard-coded hidden `source` field, plus UTM parameters | Leads counted per channel from Netlify submissions through `leadgen inbound`; site stays JS-free and strict-CSP | No automated bidding, no retargeting, no click-level attribution; stay on Manual CPC |
| 2. Plausible, cookie-free (pin 23) | Site-wide traffic and goal counts | Is JavaScript; does not feed ad bidding |
| 3. Google tag + Microsoft UET, conversion fired on /thanks/ | Automated bidding, retargeting audiences, platform-reported cost per lead | Ends no-JS rule, loosens CSP, adds cookies, needs a privacy-page rewrite |
| 4. Platform lead-form ads (LinkedIn, Meta, Reddit) | Bypasses the site and the tag question | Leads sit in each platform and must be exported; lowest-intent channels |

One fact tilts the decision: automated bidding needs roughly 15 to 30 tracked conversions before it helps, and Plan A projects 1 to 6 leads a month. The tag's headline benefit would not arrive inside the 90 days. Retargeting also needs a minimum audience that a new site will not have. **Recommendation:** run paid on Option 1 for the first 60 days. Revisit Option 3 only if search proves demand and the budget grows. Scoping the tag to /thanks/ alone would weaken attribution, because the click identifier arrives on the landing page, not the thank-you page (inference). Approval would therefore mean site-wide JavaScript, not a narrow exception. Option 1's landing pages must be noindex and kept out of the sitemap, so they do not become the thin duplicates that PartnerSlate's twin learning-center URLs illustrate ([PartnerSlate](https://partnerslate.com/learningcenter/what-is-a-copacker/)).

## The off-site footprint splits between the facility's profiles and the owner's voice

Every marketplace and supplier directory lists plants, not brokers. So the facility should own those profiles and name the owner as the inbound contact, as `docs/13_decisions.md` already recommends. Keychain gives manufacturers a free basic profile, with paid tiers reported at $5,000 to $100,000 a year in 2024; 2026 terms are unverified ([AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/from-handy-to-keychain-angi-of-cpg-tackles-biggest-pain-points-in-cpg-manufacturing)). Its process pages rank in Google, so a well-tagged profile adds search exposure ([Keychain](https://www.keychain.com/process/co-packing-services)). PartnerSlate's free manufacturer plan allows **one connection request a month with a 3% success fee** at production, and its Lite and Standard plans cost $99 and $299 a month ([PartnerSlate](http://help.partnerslate.com/en/articles/5031808-how-much-does-partnerslate-cost)). PartnerSlate has its own MSA. That 3% could stack on or conflict with Open Line's referral fee, so the referral definition (pin 10) must say whose deal a PartnerSlate-sourced brand is before the facility signs ([PartnerSlate MSA](https://partnerslate.com/msa/)). Thomasnet offers free listings to qualified North American suppliers ([Thomasnet](https://business.thomasnet.com/programs)), and the Pet Food Processing Buyers Guide has a free basic listing ([PFP](https://www.petfoodprocessing.net/media-guide/pet-food-processing-buyers-guide)). The high-trust lists founders actually read are also worth an email asking to add the facility or link an Open Line guide. They include SpecialtyFoodCoPackers' capacity page, pickyourown's master list, and extension pages such as UC Davis and UF/IFAS ([UC Davis](https://ucfoodquality.ucdavis.edu/food-industry-contacts/co-packers), [UF/IFAS](https://edis-news.ifas.ufl.edu/?p=20971)). Submission terms for these lists are unverified. Association directories such as SFA, PFI and ABA require dues, so they wait until after proof.

The owner's own footprint is where mentions come from. LinkedIn comes first: a company page, the owner's profile, and one post a week. Next is the free Startup CPG Slack, which claims direct access to 40,000 brands and experts ([Startup CPG](https://startupcpg.com/community)); the repo's older figure of 22,000 is out of date. Reddit comes after that. It is the most-cited domain across engines, so a helpful, disclosed answer there counts twice. Keep to roughly 90% genuine participation and follow each subreddit's own rules ([Redship](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules)). Which food and pet subreddits are actually active is unverified, and the repo's Reddit RSS feeds are the cheap way to check. The free press stack costs about 15 minutes a day. HARO is back under Featured.com as a free thrice-daily email. Featured's free tier allows 3 responses a month, Qwoted's free plan allows 2 pitches a month, and Source of Sources is live ([blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/seo-geo/off-page-seo-link-building/haro-alternatives-2026-featured-qwoted-and-source-of-sources), [Medialyst](https://medialyst.ai/alternatives/qwoted-alternatives)). The highest-authority free placement is a Food Dive op-ed: about 1,000 words, exclusive for 30 days, non-promotional, and **no generative AI allowed** ([Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/opinion/submit-opinion/)). The owner has to write that one personally. Podcasts are the next rung. Taste Radio takes pitches from industry experts through a form ([Taste Radio](https://www.tasteradio.com/pitch)). The Startup CPG Podcast requires a brand to be in its database first ([Startup CPG Podcast](https://startupcpg.com/podcast/)). The pet trade shows, Trending: Pet Food and More Than Kibble, may be better suited to the facility as the guest ([Petfood Industry](https://www.petfoodindustry.com/trending-pet-food-podcast)).

Four more footprint items round out the plan. First, two or three short YouTube explainers, for example on what a co-packer minimum means or how sampling works, because of the YouTube correlation. Filming the plant would reveal the facility, so that waits on pin 5. Second, Medium cross-posts of guides with a canonical link back, since ChatGPT's top sources include Medium. ## Conclusion

Open Line's competitive advantage is not its domain. It is two things incumbents cannot easily copy: a category-specific answer with honest, sourced numbers, and a live, dated statement that the lines are open. The directories that dominate search are mostly name-and-phone lists, and the pages AI engines quote for pricing and minimums are about sauce, beverages and repack work. That leaves room for a site that answers bakery and pet questions precisely. But the site cannot vouch for itself. The 90 days succeed if other domains start naming Open Line or its facility: marketplaces, extension lists, Reddit threads, a Food Dive byline, a podcast. That matters more than any ranking the site reaches by December.

The facility-fact rule, which looks like a handicap in ads and listings, turns out to support this strategy. Sourced industry ranges earn the same quoting behavior as competitors' concrete numbers, and they will not collapse if a minimum or certification changes. The largest open unknowns are search volume and whether engines discount referral sites. The owner cannot research either one away. Both come from measurement: Search Console, Bing AI Performance and a monthly prompt log for the first, and the first $300 of search clicks, largely refunded by credits, for the second.
