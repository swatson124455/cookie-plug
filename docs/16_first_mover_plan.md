# First-Mover Plan: Getting to the Tier 1 Accounts This Week

The Tier 1 accounts are small, changing, and public about it. Anyone reading the same trade press can find them. Speed matters more than polish, and none of this waits on the sending domain, the data sheet, or the referral agreement.

## Today

1. **Send from the email you already have.** Warm-up rules exist for volume. Six hand-written emails from an address you have used for years land fine. Use the custom emails in `leads/spear_list.md`; replace "[Your name]"; send Roaring Fork Mill, Legally Addictive, Fields Good, Brune Kitchen, Dog Treat Naturals, Dupe Loops, in that order. Log each with `leadgen touch <lead> 0`.
2. **Buy their product.** Roughly $150 across the six. You become a customer with a genuine reason to write a second time ("I tried the rye shortbread"), and the product is the input for the benchmark sample the moment the facility can run one.
3. **Same day, second channel.** LinkedIn connection request to the founder with a one-line note referencing the same trigger. For brands this size, also follow and comment on Instagram; founders of two-person brands read every comment.
4. **Become a small investor where a raise is live.** Legally Addictive (Vicinity), Mightylicious (Republic), and Farm to Pet (StartEngine) have open Reg CF campaigns. Minimums are typically $100 to $250; check the campaign page. Investors get updates, a Q&A channel the founder answers personally, and a legitimate opening line. Only do this with money you are fine losing.

## This week

5. **Warm intro through Startup CPG.** Roaring Fork Mill was on their podcast (episode 250) and Spoiled Pets won their SuperZoo booth. One message to the community team asking for an introduction to a founder they have already featured is a soft ask.
6. **Comment where they already are.** The podcast episode's post, the NOSH launch article, the Target listing post. A considered comment before the email arrives makes the email familiar.
7. **Google Alerts and `leadgen watch`** on each Tier 1 name so a closed raise, a new retailer, or a hire gets a same-day note from you.
8. **Reply within two hours** to anything that comes back. At this size, speed is the whole differentiator.

## What to ask the facility for (pins 16 and 17)

- **A capacity-hold letter:** for named accounts, the facility commits to reserve line time for a first production run within a stated number of weeks of an approved sample. That sentence beats every "we're booked until spring" a founder has heard, and no one else can say it.
- **A sampling commitment:** benchmark sample shipped within ten business days of receiving the product or spec. Put it in the agreement. The first email promises "sampling in weeks"; the facility has to make it true.

## What not to do

- Do not send the Bakeful email or claim any allergen line until the data sheet is back.
- Do not offer pricing. Ranges come from the facility's sheet; until then the give is the sample and the plan.
- Do not send more than these handful from your existing address. The wide net waits for the warmed domain.
