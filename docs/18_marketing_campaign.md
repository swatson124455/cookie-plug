# 90-Day Marketing Campaign

One message, everywhere a transitioning food or pet-treat founder looks: **"There is a US manufacturer with open capacity right now, and the first step is a free benchmark of your product."** Everything below repeats that in a form native to the channel. Budget for the first 90 days is close to zero; the paid layer is a decision at day 60.

## Audience and message

- **Who:** founders of emerging cookie, bakery, snack, and pet-treat brands who are outgrowing a kitchen, moving into retail, changing format, or unhappy with a first co-packer. Secondary: operations leads at established brands with a change in motion.
- **Message pillars:** (1) capacity exists now, no waitlist; (2) turnkey means you do not need a manufacturing team; (3) the proof is a sample, not a pitch; (4) small brands are welcome.
- **Voice:** plain, specific, numbers where confirmed, no hype. Founders can smell a broker.

## LinkedIn content calendar (repeat monthly, rotate specifics)

| Week | Post 1 | Post 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The capacity math: what a 2,000-door launch needs per month and why a home kitchen cannot do it | Capacity status: which lines are open this month (the same line that sits on the home page) |
| 2 | What retail buyers actually ask a new supplier (certifications, MOQ, lead time), from the facility data sheet | A founder story in the news this week (a launch, a raise) and what the production side of that looks like |
| 3 | Co-packer MOQs and why they keep rising, with a plain explanation of line economics | "Send us your product, we benchmark it": how the sample process works, step by step |
| 4 | Refrigerated to shelf-stable, or food truck to packaged: one transition explained | Reshoring: what a tariff does to an imported biscuit's landed cost |

Every post ends with one line: "If capacity is your constraint, my inbox is open." No links in the post body; the link goes in the first comment.

## Community answers (the highest-trust channel)

Rules: answer the question fully before mentioning anything; never pitch in the first reply; link a guide only when it directly answers; disclose that you represent a manufacturer. A founder who gets a straight answer about MOQs from you remembers who gave it. Target: three answers a week, tracked as leads when they turn into a DM.

## Podcast and press pitch (one paragraph, reuse)

> Emerging cookie and pet-treat brands are hitting a capacity wall: many US co-packers are booked months out and keep raising minimums, so founders who just landed Sprouts or Target cannot make the reorder. I work with a manufacturer that has open lines now and does turnkey production for small brands. Happy to talk about how founders should evaluate a co-packer, what the first call should cover, and the transitions (kitchen to co-packer, fresh to shelf-stable) that trip people up.

## Measurement

Weekly, on the same scorecard as outbound: site sessions, form submissions, community DMs, LinkedIn profile views and connection requests from food founders, referral-partner intros. Every inbound lead enters the pipeline with `source=inbound_<channel>` so the funnel shows which channel closes.

## What this campaign does not do

No paid social, no influencer spend, no trade-show booth, no gated content, no chatbot, no newsletter until there are 200 subscribers who asked for it. Each of those costs more than it returns at this stage.
