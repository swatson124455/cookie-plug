# Game Plan: Filling a Co-Packer's Open Capacity Through AI-Driven Referral Sales

**Status:** v1, built on the answers to the ten scoping questions.
**Owner:** Sam (referral partner). **Facility:** partner plant, cookies / baked goods / pet treats / pet food, ~40% open capacity, turnkey (formulation through shelf-ready).
**Commercial model:** you earn a percentage of every purchase and repurchase for roughly two years per account. You find, qualify, and warm up the buyer; the facility prices and signs.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph strategy

Contract food manufacturing is a seller's market for capacity: most US co-packers are booked six to twelve months out, push minimums up, and refuse small runs. Brands that are growing (new retail listing, sold-out products, new funding, a line extension into pet or human treats) are the ones who feel that pain today. This facility's edge is not price. It is **speed, room, and one roof**: it can sample in weeks, has open lines now, and can make a cookie and a dog biscuit for the same brand under one food-safety program. The plan is to use AI to find those growing brands at the exact moment they hit a capacity wall, reach them with a specific, evidence-based message, and hand the facility a buyer who is already convinced they need a second source. Because you are paid on repurchases, the plan prioritizes accounts that reorder monthly over one-off projects.

## 2. Why this facility wins a deal (and where it loses)

| Wins on | Why it matters to the buyer | What you must be able to say |
|---|---|---|
| Open capacity now | Their next PO cannot wait 9 months for a slot | "First sample in X days, first production run in Y weeks" |
| Turnkey | Small brands have no R&D or regulatory staff | "Formulation, packaging, labeling, nutrition panels, case packs, done" |
| Human + pet lines | Brands extending into pet (or pet brands adding human-grade) avoid a second vendor | "One plant, one audit, one PO" |
| Second-source security | Established brands got burned by a single co-packer | "Dual-source with us at 20% of volume, scale when you need to" |

| Loses on | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Unconfirmed certifications (SQF, organic, GF, kosher, AAFCO) | Get the data sheet filled in week 1. Never claim a cert you cannot document. Retail buyers will ask for the audit certificate. |
| Unknown MOQ, pricing, lead times | Pricing sheet from the partner before any first call. Without it you cannot qualify. |
| No case studies yet | Use a benchmark sample as the proof instead of a logo. "Send us your product, we match it" is a stronger proof than a testimonial. |
| You are a middleman | Be the buyer's advocate: you own the relationship until handoff and you stay in the loop. Say that out loud. |

## 3. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

Prioritize in this order. The tier drives how much time each lead gets.

### Tier A: established brands with a live capacity trigger (60% of effort)
- Revenue roughly $2M to $50M, in cookies, baked goods, snacks that could be baked, dog/cat treats, or pet food.
- Already sells through retail or wholesale, so they understand specs, MOQs, and lead times.
- **Trigger visible in public:** new national retail listing, products sold out on their site, hiring production or ops staff, recent funding, announced line extension, co-packer publicly closed or recalled, tariff-driven reshoring.
- Buying motives: capacity, second source, faster launch of a new SKU, offloading in-house production to free cash.
- Why they matter to you: they reorder monthly. One account here can be worth more than ten small ones over the two-year referral window.

### Tier B: emerging brands outgrowing their kitchen (25% of effort)
- Revenue $300k to $2M, DTC or Amazon first, often Shopify.
- Making product in a commissary or shared kitchen, no SQF, cannot get into retail without a certified facility.
- Buying motives: "I can't make enough" and "the buyer at Sprouts asked for my audit certificate."
- Risk: small first orders, may not meet MOQ. Qualify hard on volume before spending call time.

### Tier C: brand extensions and private label (15% of effort)
- Non-food brands with an audience: coffee roasters, breweries, influencers and creators, hospitality groups, sports teams, pet retailers wanting a store brand, veterinary groups, subscription boxes, corporate gifting companies, fundraising companies.
- Buying motive: a new product line with zero manufacturing knowledge. Turnkey is the whole pitch.
- Longer, less predictable cycle, but the facility does the formulation, so it captures the whole margin.

### Not worth pursuing
- Brands that state they manufacture in their own plant and show no growth signal.
- Products the facility cannot run (frozen, refrigerated, raw pet food, beverages). Confirm the line list first.
- Anything under roughly $300k revenue with no retail or wholesale traction. Nurture, do not chase.
- Regulated or gray-area functional ingredients unless the facility explicitly agrees.

## 4. Buyer personas and what each one cares about

| Persona | Typical title | What keeps them up at night | Message angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder-operator (Tier B, small Tier A) | Founder, CEO, Co-founder | Cannot keep up with orders, cannot afford a facility, retail buyer wants certifications | "You keep your brand, we solve production" |
| Operations lead (Tier A) | VP Ops, Head of Supply Chain, Director of Manufacturing | Single-source risk, co-packer lead times, MOQ creep, quality issues | "Second source with open capacity, benchmark sample in weeks" |
| Brand or product lead (Tier A/C) | Head of Product, Brand Manager, CMO | Speed to launch a new SKU or a pet line | "Formulation to shelf-ready without adding headcount" |
| Category buyer (retail private label) | Category Manager, Private Brands Director | Margin, compliance, reliability | "Certified, US-based, spare capacity for private label" |

Reach the founder at small companies and the operations lead at larger ones. Never open with the CMO at a company that has an ops team.

## 5. Trigger events: how a top salesperson times the outreach

Cold outreach that references a trigger converts several times better than outreach that does not. The engine's enrichment step looks for the first four automatically; the rest come from free alerts and searches (see `03_lead_sources.md`).

1. **Sold out or "back in stock" notices** on the brand's site (Shopify `products.json` exposes this publicly).
2. **Job postings** for production manager, co-packer manager, supply chain, QA.
3. **New retail listing** announcements ("now at Target", press, Instagram).
4. **Wholesale page or Faire listing** appears, meaning B2B demand is rising.
5. **Funding announcements** (seed, Series A, revenue-based financing).
6. **Trade show exhibitor lists** (Expo West, Sweets & Snacks, Fancy Food Show, SuperZoo, Global Pet Expo). Exhibitors are spending to grow.
7. **Competitor or co-packer trouble**: recalls, closures, bankruptcies. Every brand at that plant needs a new home immediately.
8. **Line extension signals**: a cookie brand posts about "something for your dog", a pet brand posts "human-grade".
9. **Amazon rank rising** in cookies, dog treats, or cat treats subcategories.
10. **Tariffs or import disruptions** on imported treats and biscuits pushing brands to US production.

## 7. Where AI does the work

The engine in `src/leadgen/` and the prompts in `prompts/` automate the top of the funnel. You keep the human parts: calls, judgment, and relationships.

| Step | Manual way | With this engine |
|---|---|---|
| Find leads | Hours of searching | `leadgen queries <category>` prints the exact searches; export results to CSV; `leadgen import` loads them |
| Research each company | 10 to 15 minutes per lead | `leadgen enrich` reads the homepage and Shopify catalog in seconds and records the signals |
| Decide who to contact | Gut feel | `leadgen score` ranks by ICP weights you control in `config/icp.yaml` |
| Deeper qualification | Reading and thinking | `leadgen qualify` asks Claude for fit, likely pain, disqualifiers, and a specific opening line (rule-based fallback runs free) |
| Write outreach | 20 minutes per email | `leadgen draft <lead>` renders the five-touch sequence with a lead-specific first line; `--ai` sharpens it |
| Track the pipeline | Spreadsheet drift | `leadgen advance` and `leadgen report` keep a real funnel in SQLite; `leadgen export` feeds a CRM later |
| Prep for a call | Re-reading emails | Paste the lead's facts into `prompts/qualify.md` in claude.ai for a one-page brief |
| Model the deal | Guessing | `leadgen economics` shows what an account is worth and how many contacts you need |

No API key is required to run any of this. With a key, qualification and drafting improve and cost a few cents per lead.

## 8. Messaging framework

**Positioning statement (internal):** For growing cookie, bakery, and pet treat brands that cannot get production capacity fast enough, the facility is a US turnkey manufacturer with open lines today, sampling in weeks, and human and pet products under one roof.

**Three claims you can make now** (everything else waits for the data sheet):
1. Open capacity today, so no waitlist.
2. Turnkey: formulation, production, packaging, labeling, nutrition panels, shelf-ready.
3. Cookies, baked goods, and pet treats or food in one facility.

**Claims you cannot make until confirmed:** any certification, MOQ, lead time in days, price per unit, allergen-free lines, organic.

**Rules for every message** (enforced in the templates and prompts):
- First sentence is a fact about them, not a compliment.
- One pain, in their words.
- One sentence about the facility.
- One low-friction ask: 15 minutes, or "send us your product and we will benchmark it."
- Under 110 words.

## 9. Sales process and ownership

| Stage | Exit criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| New | In the database with a website | You |
| Enriched | Signals captured | Engine |
| Qualified | Score above threshold, no disqualifier, right category | Engine + you |
| Contacted | Day-0 email sent, sequence scheduled | You |
| Replied | Any human reply | Buyer |
| Discovery call | Volume, timing, product, and decision maker confirmed (see checklist) | You |
| Sample sent | Facility has spec, sample shipped, feedback date set | Facility, chased by you |
| Handoff | Buyer has pricing, facility contact introduced, next step dated | You + facility |
| Closed won | First PO signed | Facility |
| Closed lost | Reason recorded in notes | You |

**Handoff rule:** you stay on every thread until the first PO ships. A referral partner who disappears at handoff loses deals in the last mile.

## 10. Discovery call checklist (15 minutes)

Qualify on these five. Do not send a sample without answers to the first three.
1. **Product and volume:** what SKU, current monthly units, expected units in 12 months.
2. **Timing:** when do they need the first run, and what is driving the date (PO, launch, contract ending)?
3. **Current setup:** in-house, commissary, or co-packer; what is failing.
4. **Requirements:** certifications the retailer demands, allergens, shelf life, packaging format.
5. **Decision:** who signs, who else weighs in, what does a yes need to look like.

Close the call with a dated next step: sample request, capabilities sheet, or facility call.

## 11. Metrics and targets

Funnel benchmarks used in `leadgen economics` (conservative for personalized B2B outbound):

| Stage | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Contacted to replied | 6% |
| Replied to discovery call | 40% |
| Call to sample | 50% |
| Sample to handoff | 50% |
| Handoff to closed | 50% |

Working backwards, **3 closed accounts need about 1,000 contacted leads, 60 replies, 24 calls, 12 samples, 6 handoffs.** At 25 contacts per day, five days a week, that is eight weeks of sending, which is why the roadmap front-loads list building.

Weekly scorecard (five numbers, every Friday):
- Leads added and enriched
- Day-0 emails sent
- Replies (positive / neutral / negative)
- Discovery calls held
- Samples requested

Proof milestone for the facility and for your own budget decision: **10 replies, 3 discovery calls, 1 sample request inside 30 days of sending.** Hit that and the model works; the rest is volume.

## 12. Deal economics and prioritization

Use `leadgen economics --pct <your %> --annual-purchases <their spend> --years 2` for any account. Example at 5% on an account spending $400k per year with 85% retention: about $20k in year one and $37k over two years. A Tier B brand spending $60k per year is worth about $5.5k over the same window. Ten Tier B accounts equal one and a half Tier A accounts and cost ten times the call time. Spend your hours accordingly, but keep a Tier B flow running because those are the fastest first wins for proof.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Email deliverability collapses (spam) | Warm up: 10/day week 1, 20/day week 2, 30/day after. Plain text, no links in the first email, no tracking pixels. Set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC on your domain (free). Never buy a list. |
| Facility cannot deliver on a promise you made | Only claim what is in `config/facility.yaml` with `TO_CONFIRM` removed. Get the partner on the first real call. |
| Referral terms are verbal | Get a signed referral agreement before the first handoff: percentage, term, what counts as a referred account, payment timing, audit rights, what happens if the facility already knew the brand. |
| Partner treats your leads as their own | The agreement defines "referred" as any account you logged in the pipeline before the facility's first contact. Export the pipeline weekly and email it to yourself as a timestamped record. |
| Legal: CAN-SPAM | B2B cold email is legal in the US with a real sender identity, a truthful subject, a physical address, and honoring opt-outs. The templates comply; add your address to the signature. |
| Burnout on 20 hours a week | The roadmap blocks time: 60% sending and follow-up, 25% list building, 15% calls and admin. Automation carries research. |

## 14. Tooling: zero budget now, scale later

**Now ($0):** this repo, a Gmail account on your own domain, LinkedIn free, Google Alerts, public exhibitor lists, Amazon best-seller pages, Faire's public catalog, claude.ai with the prompts in `prompts/`.
