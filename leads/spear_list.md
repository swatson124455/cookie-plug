## 1. Heavenly Hunks

**Threads**
- Casey Webber, Founder and CEO (founder thread)
- Hannah Gorski (also listed as Hannah Miller), Director of Operations (operator thread)
- Jordan Darling, Dupe Loops creator and partner, Scottsdale AZ (partner thread; the donut is his brand)

**What we know:** soft-baked GF vegan oatmeal cookies in 6 oz bags and a 22 oz organic Costco pack; ~16,000 doors across Kroger banners, Target, Walmart, Costco, Amazon; SQF Level 3 Minneapolis bakery producing "millions of cookies a day." Dupe Loops: shelf-stable glazed and frosted mini donuts, free of gluten, dairy, nuts, soy; 4 oz, $5.29; 212 Target stores in 16 states plus Target.com since Sept 21, 2026. No donut manufacturer named.

**Give:** second-source or overflow capacity for Dupe Loops if Target expands past the 212-store test; fallback, overflow cookie capacity for the 16,000-door line.

**Risk:** they may already have a donut co-manufacturer; the Target listing is still a test.

>

>

>
> [Your name]

**Operator thread (Hannah):** capacity and changeover angle: "if the cookie line is running millions a day, where do the donuts go when the test expands?" **Partner thread (Jordan):** "who bakes the loops today, and would a second US source help you sleep?"

## 2. Elavi

**Threads**
- Michelle Razavi, Co-founder and CEO (founder thread)
- Nikki Elliott, CPA, Co-founder (finance and operations thread)

**What we know:** 1.87 oz soft-baked protein brownies in three flavors, 8-ct cartons at $22.99 (Wegmans), 12-ct at Costco; plus 10 oz cashew-butter spreads. Whole Foods national with an exclusive SKU (May 28, 2026), 114 Wegmans stores, 82 Southeast Costcos, H-E-B, Amazon, Thrive, via UNFI. Bootstrapped to about $2M lifetime revenue, then a $500K pre-seed from Trousdale. Made by an unnamed US co-manufacturer; no plant. Razavi talks publicly about unit-economics discipline and declined a national Whole Foods offer once to grow regionally first.

**Give:** benchmark of the Chocolate Fudge brownie with a per-unit cost model for the 12-ct Costco pack, positioned as second-source redundancy for the Whole Foods exclusive.

**Risk:** price pressure from that unit-economics discipline; the incumbent co-man may hold a contract.

**Day-0 email, founder thread**

> Subject: second source for the whole foods sku
>
> Hi Michelle,
>
> Going national at Whole Foods seven weeks after the Wegmans launch means every reorder now runs through one manufacturer. That is the kind of single point of failure that gets expensive exactly when things are going well.
>
> I work with a US manufacturer with open baked-goods capacity right now, turnkey from formulation to shelf-ready. The pitch is not to replace your co-man. It is to have a second line that has already matched the Chocolate Fudge brownie, with a per-unit cost model you can hold against your current one, so a hiccup never costs you a Whole Foods slot.
>
> I can have that benchmark and cost sheet to you in a few weeks. Worth a 15-minute call to see if the specs line up?
>
> [Your name]

**Finance thread (Nikki):** the cost-model angle only: "a second source is cheap insurance; here is what the numbers look like."

## 3. Mightylicious Gluten Free

**Threads**
- Carolyn Haeler (also appears as Carolyn Potz), Founder and CEO. No operations or sales lead surfaced; ask her who runs supply.

**What we know:** seven GF cookie varieties in 6.5 oz bags, reformulated thicker and crispier in June 2026; also GF flours and a brownie mix. ~1,100 stores: Walmart, Costco, Whole Foods, Kroger, Fairway, King's, Price Chopper, plus Amazon, DTC, Faire. Baked "small batch, hand-crafted in New Jersey" on shared peanut, tree-nut, and soy lines (their own FAQ). A 2021 founder note describes a co-packer delaying an order. Sept 24, 2026 Republic raise funds Wakefern and Wegmans, Amazon, and "full vertical integration of its supply chain"; her quote: "building the infrastructure to serve gluten-free consumers at a national scale while protecting our margins."

**Give:** a benchmark of the Brown Butter Chocolate Chip in the new thick spec, plus a Wakefern and Wegmans volume-readiness plan. If the facility can offer an allergen-controlled line, that removes the cross-contact caveat on her FAQ; confirm before claiming.

**Risk:** "vertical integration" may mean an owned plant. Ask directly; a fast no is a good outcome.

**Day-0 email, founder thread**

> Subject: build or buy, before wakefern
>
> Hi Carolyn,
>
> I read the Republic filing. Wakefern and Wegmans on top of 1,100 doors, funded by a raise that names supply chain integration as a use of funds. One honest question: does integration mean building your own plant, or locking in capacity you control?
>
> If it is the second, I work with a US manufacturer that has open capacity now and does the whole job under one roof, formulation through nutrition panels and shelf-ready packaging, with sampling in weeks. A benchmark of the Brown Butter Chocolate Chip in the new thick spec, with a volume plan for the two new chains, is a cheap way to find out whether it belongs in your plan.
>
> If it is the first, tell me and I will get out of your way. Either answer helps me.
>
> [Your name]

## 4. Legally Addictive Foods

**Threads**
- Laura Shafferman, Co-founder (founder thread)
- Seth Eisman, Co-founder and Partner (finance thread)
- Natalie Savage, Kitchen Manager (operator thread)

**What we know:** cracker cookies (cracker base, handmade salted toffee, Belgian chocolate) in 4.7 oz bags, six to eight varieties; shelf-stable. ~2,000 retail and hospitality points: Whole Foods (43 Southeast stores), The Fresh Market, Omni, Ritz-Carlton, JetBlue Mint; Amazon, Faire, DTC. Made in-house in a 7,000 sq ft Weaverville NC kitchen; team of 12; lost ten days and accounts to Hurricane Helene, then "doubled team and space" for JetBlue. 2026 Reg CF on Vicinity, $100K to $1M at a $12M valuation, for hires, marketing, "production expansion," and working capital. The Form C states they maintain "the option to utilize qualified third-party manufacturing capacity where appropriate to supplement internal production."

**Give:** a bench of The O.G. with a per-bag cost model and a surge-capacity plan, so the raise funds sales instead of ovens.

**Risk:** handmade toffee is hard to replicate; they are proud of in-house; the raise may not close.

**Day-0 email, founder thread**

>

The offer is a benchmark you can judge blind, a per-bag cost model, and a surge plan for the JetBlue and grocery volume, so the money you raise goes to selling instead of square footage.

>
> [Your name]

## 5. Bakeful

**Threads**
- Tony Davis, Founder and CEO (former CEO of N!CK's). No other names surfaced; ask who runs supply.

**What we know:** Donutful mini donuts, Muffinful mini muffins, Brownieful mini brownies (Target exclusive); shelf-stable, OU Kosher, non-GMO, 100 percent nut-free; three fall LTOs launched Sept 24, 2026. 4,000+ doors: Target (7 to 9 SKUs, all 50 states), Walmart, HEB, Safeway, Ralphs, ShopRite, select Kroger, Amazon, DTC. Made in a "dedicated peanut- and tree-nut-free facility"; in 2023 Davis said producing donuts via a co-manufacturer "was challenging." A search snippet suggests Donutful is sold October through April because it lacks stabilizers for summer heat (unverified on the page).

**Give:** a benchmark of Brownieful or Muffinful in a shelf-stable spec that survives summer, as LTO and overflow capacity. Hard gate: the facility must confirm a nut-free line or this account is off the list.

**Risk:** the nut-free gate; SKU count and seasonality tie them to the current co-man.

**Day-0 email, founder thread (send only after the nut-free question is answered)**

> Subject: overflow for the fall ltos
>
> Hi Tony,
>
> Three fall LTOs across Target, Walmart, HEB, Kroger, and Amazon on top of the seven Target SKUs is a lot of changeovers for one plant. You said back in 2023 that making donuts through a co-manufacturer was harder than it looked. I would like to hear whether that is still true.
>
> I work with a US manufacturer that has open baked-goods capacity now and runs formulation, packaging, labeling, and nutrition panels in-house. First question is allergen setup, which I will answer before we go any further. If it fits, a benchmark of Muffinful in a spec that holds up in July is the give.
>
> Worth 15 minutes to compare notes on what a second line would need?

> [Your name]
